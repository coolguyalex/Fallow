# Fallow

A Playdate game about ecological succession in an abandoned New England field.
Seeds blow in, take root, and grow. Insects arrive once there is plant life;
birds follow the insects and drop berry seeds. The field slowly changes while
you mostly watch.

**Status: early. Climate engine complete and validated. No plants yet.**

---

## The crank is time

The first day the game is run on your playdate marks time 0. 
After that, turning the crank advances the clock — but never past the real one. Each
session you crank from where you left off up to now. Play once a month and you
crank through a month of weather, seed dispersal and visits. 

This gives the illusion of running through time that has elapsed. However, this is not playback and The field's state at any moment is a deterministic
function of `(fieldSeed, timestamp)`, so there is no background simulation and
no saved world. A year of absence costs nothing at runtime; it is rendered on
demand.

You can collect wind-blown seeds, uproot plants, and bank specimens. You cannot
obtain seeds any other way, control the weather, or rewind. **Any intervention
locks that timeline permanently** — every choice is a bet placed without seeing
the rest of the year, no rewind and undo. 

## What runs today

- Crank-driven clock, calendar, and a 24-hour readout
- Sun and moon on arcs, setting behind the horizon
- Day/night via hardware pixel inversion — one set of drawing code, both states
- Rain and snow at the right hour, hiding the sun and stars
- A climate model fitted to Providence, Rhode Island
- Scene brightness derived from true solar elevation
- Growing degree-days accumulating, waiting for something to grow

## Build

Requires the [Playdate SDK](https://play.date/dev/) with its `bin` directory
on your `PATH`.

```
git clone https://github.com/coolguyalex/Fallow.git
cd Fallow
pdc Source Fallow.pdx
```

Then double-click `Fallow.pdx`, or drag it onto the Playdate Simulator. To run
on hardware, use the Simulator's **Device → Upload Game to Device**.

`pdc`'s first argument is whatever folder contains `main.lua`. `Source` is
convention, not a requirement.

## The climate model

The weather is not decoration and it is not random. It is fitted to real data
for Providence, Rhode Island, and validated against it.

| | Providence | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Annual mean temperature | 10.6–10.9 °C | 10.95 |
| Worst monthly error | — | 0.80 °C |
| Day-to-day scatter, winter | ~5.5 °C | 5.36 |
| Day-to-day scatter, summer | ~3.0 °C | 3.06 |
| Wet days per year | ~123 | 125 |
| Annual rainfall | 1151 mm | 1183 |
| Solstice daylight | 15h 15m | 15h 03m |

The seasonal curve is a least-squares Fourier fit to the monthly normals. One
harmonic turned out to be enough — a second carried 0.27 °C of amplitude and
barely moved the residual, because Providence's annual cycle is very nearly a
pure sinusoid. The July lag emerges from the fit rather than being imposed.

Daily variation is the interesting part. White noise is wrong: real cold snaps
last four days, not one, and daily temperature anomalies in New England have a
lag-1 autocorrelation around 0.70. So the anomalies come from **five octaves of
value noise at 20, 10, 5, 2.5 and 1 day periods, with equal weight** — which is
to say, pink noise.

The weights were solved for rather than chosen. Equal weight per octave lands
at a measured 0.71. And it works for a reason that needs no Fourier theory: a
slow wiggle physically cannot change much in a day, because it needs its whole
period to complete a swing. Each band carries its own stickiness —

| Period | Its lag-1 | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 20 days | 0.996 | a month-long regime |
| 10 days | 0.985 | a spell |
| 5 days | 0.943 | one weather system passing |
| 2.5 days | 0.790 | the front arriving and leaving |
| 1 day | 0.063 | all sub-daily physics not modelled |

The sum's autocorrelation is the variance-weighted average of that column:
predicted 0.7141, measured 0.7167.

There is no memory anywhere in the code. Four sticky signals mixed with one
non-sticky one produced weather that remembers. That is Wiener–Khinchin doing
its work: a power spectrum and an autocorrelation function are Fourier
transforms of one another, so shaping the spectrum and shaping the memory are
the same act.

Readers from audio will recognise the construction as essentially the
**Voss–McCartney pink noise algorithm**, clocked at one sample per day instead
of forty-four thousand per second.

Every one of those numbers is regenerated by the validation harness rather
than typed.

## Design notes

`HANDOFF.md` carries the settled decisions and the reasoning behind them —
why real New England species over invented ones, why uprooting is disturbance
management rather than gardening, why the day is the atomic unit of
simulation, and which questions are still open.

`BACKLOG.md` is the running feature list. Nothing in it is scheduled.
